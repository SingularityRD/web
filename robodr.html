Why RoboDR

Six reasons to choose RoboDR

01 · Who commands it

Know who is giving your robot orders

On most robots, motion commands cross the network without any check, so anyone on that network can tell the robot to walk or raise an arm. RoboDR reads every command sent to the motors, knows which senders belong and flags a stranger at once.

  • Reads Unitree and ROS 2 motion, arm and hand commands
  • Flags unknown senders, even when they change identity
  • Catches spoofed, replayed or out-of-limit speed and joint commands
  • Compares what the robot was told with what it did
  • Spots faked or frozen telemetry and disagreeing navigation sensors

02 · Break-ins

Catch a break-in before it reaches the motors

In 2025 a published vulnerability let attackers take over widely used robots over Bluetooth alone. From there they install software, change the robot’s apps or switch off defenses. RoboDR watches the robot’s onboard computer and raises an alert at every step of known robot attacks.

  • New processes, outbound connections and remote tunnels
  • Package installs and changes to ROS 2 and robot apps
  • Bluetooth and Wi-Fi setup tools and wireless setting changes
  • Reads of the robot’s private keys
  • Any attempt to change or stop RoboDR itself

03 · Safe stop

Stop the attack without dropping the robot

For a humanoid or four-legged robot, cutting the motors means a fall. RoboDR blocks dangerous commands before they arrive and keeps humanoid and four-legged robots on their feet. The response escalates in stages, so a moving robot is never stopped blindly.

  • Every command passed, limited or rejected
  • Escalates in stages: alert, restrict, slow down, pause, safe state
  • Slows the robot without changing its direction
  • Stop commands are never delayed
  • Isolates the robot or quarantines a rogue sender

04 · People decide

Your people make the calls that matter

An automatic action on a moving robot is a risk of its own. RoboDR’s rules limit dangerous commands on their own, but pausing the robot, a safe state or a rollback needs two people. Every action is time-limited and can be undone.

  • Starts monitor-only; protection goes on when you are ready
  • Learns each robot’s normal, then locks it with human sign-off
  • The approver is always someone other than the requester
  • Emergency off at any time
  • Asks a person whenever the robot’s state is unknown

05 · Your SOC

Robot incidents land where your team already works

A robot alert in a separate tool gets missed. Each RoboDR finding opens a tracked case in AutoSecOps, Singularity’s AI security operations product, next to your other alerts. An AI agent triages it and recommends a response. A person decides.

  • A case opens for every finding, before an analyst looks
  • The AI cannot delete cases, run scripts or go online
  • Attacker text from the robot never becomes AI instructions
  • Forged, stale or unsigned findings rejected and logged
  • Whole fleet on one screen, with each robot’s cases

06 · Proof

Answer the auditor with proof, not memory

After an incident someone will ask what happened and who intervened. RoboDR writes every incident to a tamper-proof black box, like a flight recorder. Auditors can check the records but cannot forge them, which supports your evidence for the Machinery Regulation and the Cyber Resilience Act.

  • Records survive power loss; any deletion shows
  • Every finding mapped to MITRE ATT&CK and tied to evidence
  • Pull evidence by finding or time range into the case
  • Findings in the open OCSF format, for any compatible SIEM
  • Signed releases with a software bill of materials (SBOM)

Straight talk

What RoboDR will not do

Replace your safety system

RoboDR is not a safety-rated component. It complements the robot’s own safety system and never stands in for it.

Cut power or reset e-stops

It never cuts motor power, resets the emergency stop or kills a program on a moving robot. With the e-stop active, it only records.

Pause a robot on its own

Rules limit dangerous commands automatically. Pausing the robot, a safe state or a rollback always needs two people’s approval.

Certify you

Its documents for IEC 62443 and the Cyber Resilience Act are an engineering self-assessment, not a certification.

Questions buyers ask

Questions about RoboDR

What is RoboDR, and which robots does it work with?

RoboDR is a security layer for robots from Singularity. It sees an attack as it begins, stops dangerous commands before they reach the motors and keeps a tamper-proof record. It supports Unitree quadruped and humanoid robots, and robots running ROS 2, the open-source robot software.

Can RoboDR run on our own servers, at a site with no internet?

Yes. RoboDR’s security center runs on your own servers or in your cloud, and RoboDR works at air-gapped sites, meaning sites with no internet connection. No component calls out, and your data stays on your infrastructure.

Will RoboDR take action on our robots without asking us?

RoboDR starts in monitor-only mode, and protection goes on only when you are ready. Its rules limit dangerous commands automatically. Pausing a robot, a safe state or a rollback needs approval from two people, the approver is never the requester, and every action can be undone.

Does RoboDR replace or interfere with our robot’s safety system?

No. RoboDR is not a safety-rated component; it complements the robot’s own safety system. It never cuts motor power, resets the emergency stop or kills a program on a moving robot, and it keeps humanoid and four-legged robots from falling during a stop.

Our IT security already covers the network. Why do we need RoboDR?

Traditional security software protects the computer. RoboDR protects the robot. It reads the commands sent to the motors, can tell “walk” from “run”, and stops a hijacked robot without a fall. Its findings land in your existing SOC, beside your other alerts.
