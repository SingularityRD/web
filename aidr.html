Questions about AIDR

What is AIDR?

AIDR is Singularity’s security product for AI agents, the AI assistants that open files, run programs and connect to servers by themselves. AIDR watches what each agent actually does, blocks harmful actions before they run and keeps a record of every decision.

Who is AIDR for?

AIDR is for companies whose AI agents touch real systems: developers using coding agents, teams building their own agents, and regulated organizations such as banks, hospitals, defense and government bodies that must show what their AI did.

How is AIDR different from a prompt firewall?

A prompt firewall only reads the text an agent receives and writes. AIDR also checks what the agent does before it runs: the programs it starts, the files it touches and the connections it opens. On Linux, kernel-level enforcement is available as a preview.

Can AIDR run on-premises or air‑gapped?

Yes. AIDR runs on your own servers and on air-gapped networks, which are closed to the outside world. Decisions are made on your own machines, and protection stays on even if the internet connection goes down. Supported operating systems are listed in the technical brief.

How do people stay in control of AIDR?

You set the rules. AIDR lets everyday work through, sends irreversible actions such as deleting a database to a person for approval, and blocks harmful ones. A tricked agent cannot approve its own action, and every decision is logged.
